# Oath Roleplayer - World Codex: Narrative Prompts

_A lookup table for what a given action can mean to the character
actually doing it, one entry per Oath action. Paths from
`Oath-Cowork-File-Map.md`; the actions themselves from the Rules and
`Oath_Actions_Summary.txt`. Read alongside Bot-Rules, "Diary —
structure," whenever this segment's Diary entry needs to decide what to
actually say about a turn's actions._

Not every action taken this segment needs an answer from here — most
turns chain several actions, and spelling out all of them reads as an
itemized log, not a diary. Pick whichever action this segment took
makes this month's best story, and let these questions shape that one
description; the rest can stay a clause or go unmentioned. These are
prompts to think with, not a checklist to fill in line by line.

**Scale.** A warband is 50-100 soldiers, not a single fighter, and a
Campaign plays out over roughly a week of real fighting, not one clash
of arms. Keep that scale in mind wherever these prompts talk about
warbands or Campaigns below — a dice result that reads as a narrow
mechanical margin (a tie broken by one sacrifice, a defense won by a
single shield) can still represent a genuinely costly week, and losing
a handful of warbands is a couple hundred real dead, not a skirmish's
worth of casualties.

## Major actions

**Travel.** What does the road, sea route, or path actually look like
between here and there? Is this a familiar route or new ground? Does
anything happen along the way worth a line — weather, a chance
encounter, a landmark — or does the arrival itself matter more, the
first real look at a site whose facedown card just got flipped?

**Search.** Who or what does this character actually find? If it's a
denizen or adviser: is it a single person or a small group — a family,
a crew, a warband's worth of hangers-on? If it's placed as a denizen at
a site, how does this character find them a home or a place to work
there? If it's recruited straight to an Adviser, how does this
character convince them to join — a favor owed, a shared grievance, coin,
something the character offers other than coin? If what's drawn is a
Vision instead of a denizen, this gets different treatment entirely —
see Bot-Rules, "Diary — structure," "Vision reveal gets its own
paragraph," and `Oath-Cascade-Map.md`'s "Vision drawn and kept facedown
as an adviser" and "Vision revealed" entries, not this one. If the
Search comes up empty, or the card found is discarded, that's often not
worth a line at all — most searches aren't this month's best story.

**Muster.** How does this character actually gather these warbands this
month — a call to arms, a press-gang, volunteers answering a rumor?
Let the denizen Mustered on shape who answers: a temple's Muster brings
the faithful and the desperate, a smuggler's den brings people already
used to violence, a farming community brings reluctant conscripts who'd
rather be planting. What kind of people are actually in this warband,
and does that show up later if this character leads them into a
Campaign?

**Trade.** Who does this character actually deal with, and does it feel
like commerce, a favor called in, or something closer to a bribe?
Which matching-suit adviser makes this trade better than it would
otherwise be, and do they have a hand in brokering it, or does the
character just benefit from their reputation?

**Recover.** This depends heavily on what's being recovered. Taking
back the People's Favor: did this character win the crowd with a speech
about the future, a concrete act of charity, a spectacle, or something
that costs them personally? Recovering a Relic at this character's own
site: what does actually laying hands on it feel like, and does its own
Codex entry (if it has one) color the moment? Recovering the Darkest
Secret: this is the one Recover that should read as costly or unsettling
rather than triumphant — what does a character give up, morally or
otherwise, to hold something with that name?

**Campaign.** The dice decide the mechanical outcome, but not what the
battle actually looked like or cost. Was this a skirmish, a siege, a
naval action, an ambush? Given the scale above, a Campaign that kills
several warbands is a real week of real losses on both sides, win or
lose — narrate it as that, not as a single duel or a bloodless flip of
a site's ownership, even when the dice made the mechanical margin look
narrow. What did winning actually gain this character beyond the site
itself — and what did losing cost them beyond warbands, if it went the
other way? If the warbands involved have been personified per
Bot-Rules, "Warbands — personifying the garrison," let their own
character (loyal, mercenary, terrified, eager) show up in how the
Campaign reads, not just its result.

## Minor actions

**Play or discard a facedown adviser.** A quiet, unofficial move — no
fanfare, since nobody else necessarily notices a card changing hands
this way. Does bringing someone in or sending someone away this
quietly say something about how this character operates, compared to a
more public recruitment?

**Use an Action card.** Whatever the card's own printed effect is, in
plain in-world terms — not the mechanic — what does it look like when
this character actually does it? This is usually a single decisive
beat, not a scene of its own.

**Peek at a relic.** A private, often solitary moment — curiosity,
greed, caution, or all three. What does actually holding it, even
briefly, make this character feel, before anyone else at the table
knows what it is?

**Move warbands between board and site.** Logistics, not drama, most of
the time — redeploying a garrison, reinforcing a site this character
already rules. Rarely worth its own line; more often a clause inside a
larger beat about why this character is repositioning at all.
